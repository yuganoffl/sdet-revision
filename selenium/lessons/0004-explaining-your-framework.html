Lesson 04 · Selenium

Explaining your framework

Two questions, one structure. "Tell me about your framework" and "how would you build one from scratch" are the same six layers, told forwards or backwards. Learn the layers and you can answer either, plus every follow-up they hang off them.

Read this first. It decides everything else on the page.

You have not built a Selenium framework yet. You start on Monday. What you have built is the TestNG layer in this repo, and that is real, and it is yours.

So do not open with "in my framework". Open with what is true. A borrowed story survives one question and collapses on the second, because the follow-ups are always "why did you choose that" and "what went wrong with it". You cannot invent those.

The honest version is not the weak version. Read the script below before you decide it costs you anything.

The six layers

Every Selenium framework worth the name is these six, whatever the company calls them. Learn them in order. They are the spine of both answers, the map of the repo, and the list of follow-up questions.

#LayerIts one jobKey piece
1ConfigurationNo value is hard-codedlayered properties, -D wins
2DriverMake a browser, safely, in parallelDriverFactory + ThreadLocal
3PagesHide the DOM from the testspage objects, BasePage
4DataTest data lives outside the codeExcel via POI, @DataProvider
5TestsRead like the user's journeyTestNG, BaseTest, groups
6ReportingDiagnose a failure without rerunninglisteners, screenshots, retry, Allure

Layers 1 and 2 are setup. Layer 3 is where the design taste shows. Layer 6 is what separates a framework from a folder of tests.

The one rule that ties them together

Say this sentence and stop talking for a second

"Nothing in my tests package imports Selenium. No By, no WebElement, no WebDriver. If a test needs one, my page object is missing a method."

It is short, it is checkable, and most candidates cannot say it about their own repo. Selenium's own guidance agrees: page objects "should seldom expose the underlying WebDriver instance".

The 90 second script, honest version

Four beats. Do not memorise the words, memorise the four beats and the one number in each.

Beat 1 — where you actually are (about 15 seconds)

"I have built the TestNG side of a framework in my own practice repo, and I am building the Selenium layer on top of it now. So let me tell you what is running today, and then how I am putting the rest together."

That is one sentence and it buys you the whole rest of the answer. You are now describing, not claiming.

Beat 2 — what is running today (about 30 seconds)

"TestNG driving it, with suite XMLs for selection. Tests tagged into groups so the pre-merge run is the smoke slice and the nightly is everything. Data comes out of Excel through Apache POI into a @DataProvider — I wrote that reader, and I found a bug in it where a blank row in the middle of a sheet silently dropped every row after it, because it used getPhysicalNumberOfRows instead of getLastRowNum. Parallel execution with a ThreadLocal, and a listener layer with a retry analyser."

All of that is true and all of it is yours. The POI bug is the strongest thing in the answer, because nobody invents a debugging story that specific.

Beat 3 — the Selenium design (about 30 seconds)

"The Selenium layer is a driver factory returning Chrome, Firefox or Edge behind the WebDriver interface, held in a ThreadLocal so a parallel run cannot cross the streams. Page objects with plain By constants and explicit waits, not PageFactory. Explicit waits everywhere and the implicit wait left at zero, because mixing them makes the timeout unpredictable — I measured that: a four second implicit wait plus a six second explicit wait timed out at eight and a half seconds."

That measurement is your credibility. It is a number from your own machine.

Beat 4 — failures (about 15 seconds)

"On failure the listener takes a screenshot and logs the URL, the browser and the data row, so I can diagnose without rerunning. Retry is deliberate and visible rather than silent — and one thing I learned there is that a retried attempt fires onTestSkipped, not onTestFailure, so a naive screenshot-on-failure listener captures nothing for the attempts you most want to see."

Then stop. Do not keep talking. The follow-up question is where you win, and you have just laid out four of them.

"How would you build one from scratch?"

Same six layers, in build order, with the reason attached to each. The reason is the answer; the step is just the peg it hangs on.

  1. Skeleton and one smoke test. Maven, pinned versions in one properties block, one test that opens a browser and asserts a title. Why first: it proves the setup on a clean machine before any design decisions can hide a broken build.
  2. Config. Layered. A checked-in default, an environment file, then -D on the command line beating both. Why early: every layer after this wants a URL, a browser and a timeout, and if you do not build it now you will scatter System.getProperty through the whole codebase. No secrets in the repo, ever.
  3. Driver factory and driver manager. A browser enum, per-browser options, and a ThreadLocal<WebDriver> behind a private field. Why here: parallel safety is impossible to retrofit. Bolt it on later and you rewrite every page object.
  4. Page objects. A BasePage owning the driver and every wait, then one page object per page, plus components for the header and modals that appear everywhere. Enforce the no-Selenium-in-tests rule from the first page object, not from the fiftieth.
  5. Data layer. POI wrapped so tests never touch a POI type, rows mapped to objects, wired into @DataProvider. Why wrapped: the day you move from Excel to CSV or a database, one class changes.
  6. Listeners and reporting. ITestListener for logging and screenshot-on-failure, IRetryAnalyzer for deliberate retries, and a real report with the screenshot attached. Why last: it is the layer that needs all the others to exist, and it is the one that makes the suite usable by somebody who is not you.
  7. Then CI. Runs on every push, publishes the report as an artifact, a matrix across browsers.
The sentence that makes this answer sound senior

"My test for whether it is done is: a new engineer clones it, runs one command, and is productive by Wednesday. If they need a wiki page of setup steps, I have not finished it."

That reframes the question from "which tools" to "who has to live with this", which is what the interviewer is really probing.

The follow-ups, and one line each

They askYou say
Why ThreadLocal?Two threads share one instance, so they share a plain field and one overwrites the other. ThreadLocal gives each thread its own driver.
Why not PageFactory?Selenium's own page object guidance never mentions it, and its lazy proxies compose badly with explicit waits. Plain By constants are clearer.
How do you handle waits?Explicit everywhere, implicit left at zero. Mixing them makes the timeout the explicit one plus up to one implicit wait.
How do you do cross-browser?A factory returning three drivers behind the WebDriver interface. One config value changes; no test or page object knows.
How do you manage drivers?Selenium Manager. Built in since 4.6, so no WebDriverManager and no System.setProperty.
How do you handle flaky tests?Find the real cause first, usually a wait. IRetryAnalyzer for genuinely non-deterministic ones, and the retry is visible in the report, never silent.
Which reporting tool?Allure. ExtentReports was sunset by its own author, and Allure survives a change of test runner.
How do you check five things in one test?SoftAssert, then assertAll(). A hard assert stops at the first failure and hides the other four.
How is data separated?Excel through POI, wrapped so tests never see a POI type, into a @DataProvider. A non-programmer can add a row.
What is StaleElementReference?You found the element, then the page replaced it. The handle points at a node no longer in the DOM. Re-find it; a longer wait does not help.
Three answers that lose you the room

"We use Thread.sleep where it's flaky." Slower and less reliable than waiting properly. This is the single thing interviewers listen for.

"We use ExtentReports." Fine to have used it, but know it was sunset by its own author. Naming it as a current choice dates you.

"We use WebDriverManager." Unnecessary since Selenium 4.6. Same problem: it says you learned from tutorials written in 2019.

Check yourself

You are asked about your framework and you have built part of one. What do you open with?

Why build the ThreadLocal driver manager early rather than later?

One test needs to check five fields on a confirmation page. How?

A test throws StaleElementReferenceException. What happened?

Tonight

  1. Say the four beats out loud, timed. Ninety seconds. If you run over, cut beat 3, not beat 2, because beat 2 is the part that is provably yours.
  2. Cover the right column of the follow-up table and work down the left. Any you cannot answer in one sentence, ask me now.
  3. Reread the two numbers you own: the mixed-wait measurement from lesson 2, and the POI getPhysicalNumberOfRows bug. Those two are what make the rest sound like experience rather than reading.
